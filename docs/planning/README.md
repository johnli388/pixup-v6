## Recommended product direction

Build a multi-sided collectibles SaaS platform with a shared asset registry and transaction core. Buyers collect and trade; sellers list inventory and manage fulfillment; IP creators publish licensed collections and view attribution and earnings. Certification, custody, provenance, settlement, and recirculation support every channel.

The strongest differentiator in the documents is the continuity of an individual physical collectible's history through issuance, certification, sale, custody, and recirculation. A marketplace alone would miss this requirement. Conversely, building every channel and every AI feature at once would delay validation of the core business.

User-selected delivery sequence: web marketplace and invited creators first, then CVM and LiveUP pilots. Retain a provisional 20–24-week remaining envelope to controlled launch, physical/live pilots during months 7–9, and broader SaaS and recirculation operations during months 10–12+. Re-estimate after two delivery cycles. This deliberately changes the documents' channel-first sequencing to address the requested creator/seller/buyer platform. The sequence is confirmed; dates, staffing, vendors, and unresolved commercial policies remain estimates or proposals.

BP p. 27 proposes card shows/GlowUP before CVM rollout, and p. 28 describes early GOUP experiments. Research and approved event-tagged manual validation may run alongside the web pilot; full group-funding, wholesale, live and device products still require their commercial/operational gates. BP p. 10's longer issuance progression—agency first, licensing around year 1.5, platform in early year 2 and own-brand around year 2.5—is a scenario, not authorization to activate all issuance modes now.

The first release supports physical sports cards and K-pop photocards. Model category-specific attributes so toys and other collectibles can follow after authentication, packaging, and condition standards exist for each category. Digital certificates represent records about physical items; they are not NFTs or separately tradable assets.

Each metric needs an owner, version, eligible events/population, period/as-of date, unit, numerator/denominator and correction treatment. Distinguish observed results from forecasts; show unavailable when a denominator or valuation is undefined. Keep GMV, gross receipts, owned-stock margin, service revenue, subscription MRR/ARR, liabilities, credits, VACV and actual funded reserves separate. Verified external trades can inform price references without increasing PixUP GMV. Demo/test records never populate live metrics.

## Team and budget assumptions

The 24-week estimate assumes approximately 5–6 full-time-equivalent product/engineering contributors: one technical lead/backend engineer, one additional backend engineer, two frontend/full-stack engineers, one product designer/PM, and fractional QA/platform capacity increasing near release. Finance, VIA reviewers, warehouse operations, legal, and customer support are additional dependencies. The six founders listed in the decks are business owners, not six assumed full-time software engineers.

Use the BP's $750,000 protocol-stack allocation as a budget constraint, not a vendor quote. One illustrative 12-month envelope is $430,000 engineering, $90,000 product/design/QA, $45,000 cloud and engineering services, $45,000 security and integration validation, and $140,000 contingency. This requires a blended hiring model and staged staffing; a full senior US team for a year could exceed it. Avoid double-counting the separate founder-payroll budget. Hardware, inventory, legal fees, shipping, PSP transaction fees, and warehouse labor belong to other operating budgets. Re-estimate after two delivery cycles using actual rates and throughput.

With only 2–3 engineers, narrow the initial release to platform-owned inventory, invited creator drafts, manual certification and finance operations, and one payment route; rebaseline the schedule rather than retaining the entire feature list.

## Next two delivery cycles

1. Product and finance decide launch categories, allowed markets/ages, custody terms, seller payout rules, fees, and the limited role of credits.
2. Creator operations define rights evidence, moderation, edition approval, manufacturing handoff, and what “published” means before stock exists.
3. VIA and warehouse teams define capture standards, physical identifiers, custody handoffs, exception handling, and condition reassessment.
4. Engineering extends the existing Stripe test foundation: exercise hosted payment/refund, design exception resolution and recipient onboarding/payout, validate business eligibility and run migrations 004–005 against external PostgreSQL. Preserve current idempotency and simulation guards.
5. Design tests buy, publish, sell and recirculate flows with representative users, including source registration, changed-offer consent, payment review, physical custody and partner statements. These tests refine the scoped backlog.
6. Finance/governance define the metric dictionary, resource contracts and own-issuance cap. Backend B0/B1/B2/B4/B6 and frontend F0/F1/F2 then deliver a staging evidence/payment-recovery slice using existing invitations, staff scopes and offer history.

Success means a real collectible can move through a complete, explainable, reconciled lifecycle. Growth, repeat purchases, creator activation, and circulation are measured alongside dispute rates, authenticity failures, inventory accuracy, and operating cost; transaction count alone is not success.
